### Verdict
- **Consensus:** No legal duty to act as a bystander (Title VII/EPA bind the employer, not you). Staying silent indefinitely isn't a neutral option either. And two moves need nobody's permission: being transparent about your own pay, and writing down the HR timeline now.
- **Strongest dissent:** Pragmatist wants documented escalation this week; Skeptic and Critic want verification + the affected colleagues' consent first, with escalation conditional on what they actually want. The fight is over sequencing, not destination.
- **Premise check:** Yes — Skeptic challenged the framing twice: "no visible action" may not mean "nothing's happening" (comp remediation is often slow and invisible by design), and the built-in "ally escalates on their behalf" script assumes a mandate nobody's confirmed.
- **Recommendation:** (1) Calibrate confidence — is this hard data or inference? (2) Talk to one affected colleague privately: do they know, do they want backup, what would actually help — this resolves the consent question that decides everything else. (3) Do the two zero-permission moves now regardless: be open about your own comp if it's useful to them, and get the HR timeline in writing to yourself, dated. (4) If they want an ally and HR is still inert, escalate above HR in writing — legal/compliance, skip-level, or ethics hotline — as the *next* step, not the opening one. (5) Formal complaints (EEOC/state agency, attorney) are theirs to file, not yours — your role is pointing them to an employment attorney, not filing for them.
