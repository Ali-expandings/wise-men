## Council: Market Entry Against a Dominant Incumbent

**Architect:** Go niche — but only where the incumbent is structurally boxed out (bad margins, wrong workflow, brand mismatch at their scale), then expand from that beachhead.
A broad fight plays to their resource edge; a structurally-ignored segment is the one place their scale doesn't help them.

**Skeptic:** The real choice isn't "unhappy customers vs. niche" — it's which structural asymmetry you own that they can't copy without hurting themselves; let that pick your customers for you.
Otherwise you're choosing a battlefield at random, and an "ignored" niche may just be one they haven't gotten to yet.

**Pragmatist:** Take the structurally-ignored niche, then win on shipping cadence — out-executing their slower decision cycle matters more than which segment you picked.
Unhappy customers are rented, not owned: one fix from a well-funded incumbent and they're gone; a segment that's genuinely uneconomical for the incumbent stays yours even after they notice.

**Critic:** Same niche call, but treat it as a countdown, not a stable position — the moment you prove it works, you become visible to their board, so lock-in has to exist before that happens, not after.
"Ignored" hides two different cases — permanently uneconomical for them vs. just not-yet-prioritized — and most failures never diagnose which one they're actually in.

### Verdict
- **Consensus:** All four voices reject "chase unhappy customers" as the core strategy — dissatisfaction rarely means switching intent, and it's the cheapest thing for a 10x-funded incumbent to patch once you've proven it matters. All converge on a niche play, specifically one the incumbent avoids for structural/economic reasons, not one that's merely currently unserved.
- **Strongest dissent:** They disagree on what the real lever is once you're in the niche. Skeptic: find the structural asymmetry first, segment second. Pragmatist: shipping cadence beats segment choice. Critic: the niche is a time-boxed phase, and lock-in built before the incumbent reacts is what actually decides the outcome. Read these as three simultaneous requirements, not competing answers.
- **Premise check:** Yes — the Skeptic rejected the question's own framing, arguing "unhappy customers vs. ignored niche" isn't the real decision; the real decision is which structural asymmetry to build the business around.
- **Recommendation:** Don't make unhappy customers the primary strategy — treat any interest from them as a bonus signal, not a plan; they're the easiest thing for a well-resourced incumbent to win back. Find a niche the incumbent ignores for structural reasons — their cost structure, workflow, or brand makes it genuinely unprofitable or awkward for them, not just currently unaddressed. Win it fast by out-shipping their slower decision cycle, then spend the resulting window deliberately building switching costs and lock-in before your traction becomes visible enough for them to respond. That combination — structural niche, speed, pre-emptive lock-in — is the "something else": a disciplined niche play, not a segment pick alone.
