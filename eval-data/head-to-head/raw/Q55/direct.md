Short answer: yes, there's a real version of this — but only under specific conditions, and it's worth being honest about which ones apply to you.

**The case where it makes sense**

A PhD at 35, leaving a good salary, without wanting academia, is rational when:

1. **The PhD is the only path to the specific capability you want**, not just credentials. If what you're curious about requires the apprenticeship — deep mentorship, lab access, years of sustained focus on one hard problem — and you can't get that any other way (reading papers, a part-time master's, an industry research role), that's a real reason.
2. **You have a concrete use for it that isn't "professor."** Industry research labs, technical leadership roles, founding something deep-tech, policy work that needs the credential to be taken seriously — these all sometimes require or heavily reward the PhD without requiring the academic career track.
3. **You can afford the shape of the loss**, not just the stipend gap. 4-6 years at PhD pay, plus the compounding you miss on salary, equity, retirement contributions, and career momentum. If you run the numbers and can absorb it without wrecking your options at 41, that's different from "I'll figure it out."
4. **You've tested the curiosity against the actual work, not the idea of it.** Have you done anything that resembles PhD work — a research project, published something, worked closely with academics, sat in on a lab? Liking the idea of research and liking the reality of research (rejection, narrow scope, years on one question, writing) are different things.

**Where the romanticizing shows up**

- Wanting the identity ("I'm someone who does deep intellectual work") more than the actual six-year grind of a specific dissertation topic.
- Treating it as an exit from a job you don't like, dressed up as intellectual pursuit — worth checking if the real driver is "I want out" rather than "I want in."
- Not having asked: could I get 70% of this curiosity satisfied by reading, a part-time program, or a sabbatical/leave rather than a full PhD?

**What I'd actually do before deciding:** talk to 3-4 people who did a PhD in the relevant field in their 30s, left academia, and are now doing something you'd want to be doing. Ask what the PhD specifically bought them that they couldn't have gotten cheaper. And try writing an actual research proposal — the thing you'd propose in an application — and see if it excites you as a five-year grind, not just a two-paragraph idea.
