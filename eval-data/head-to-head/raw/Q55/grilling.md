**Final recommendation**

There's a version of this where going back makes sense — but it isn't "resign and start a 5-7 year PhD on the strength of a feeling." The version that holds up:

1. **Test it before you commit.** Don't quit yet. Get inside real research at low cost and short time horizon: collaborate with a lab/PI, do a part-time or research-track master's, take an unfunded RA stint, or negotiate a leave of absence. Six to twelve months of this turns "curious" from a feeling into evidence, for a fraction of the cost of a wrong five-year bet.

2. **If the test confirms real appetite for the daily grind** (not just the idea of it), pursue the full PhD only if: it's fully funded — don't self-fund tuition on top of lost salary; you pick a program/advisor with a real non-academic placement track record, since academia is still an open question for you; and you go in treating it as a possible identity-shift, not a résumé line, because the finances alone (a six-figure-plus opportunity cost against a diffuse payoff) don't clear the bar on ROI terms for someone already in a well-paying job.

3. **If the cheap test instead reveals this is mostly about escaping a job that's stopped feeling meaningful, or wanting the scholar identity back** — don't do the full PhD. The actual fix is smaller: a job change, a sabbatical, or a part-time credential, not five years of your life. That's the version that would be romanticizing it.

Bottom line: yes, there's a real version of this — but it starts with a cheap, reversible test of the actual work, not a resignation letter.
