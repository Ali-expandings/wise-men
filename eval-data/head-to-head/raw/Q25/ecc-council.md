## Council: Why Psychology Replications Fail (p-hacking vs. something deeper)

**Architect:** It's not p-hacking retroactively exposed so much as p-hacking retroactively exposing a knowledge-production process that was never built to survive scrutiny — weak, direction-only theory plus a near-universal "crud factor" of ambient correlation let flexible analysis produce plausible-looking confirmations almost on demand.
Preregistration lifting replication rates to ~70-80% proves the QRP layer was real and fixable; the leftover gap, and the uneven replication across subfields, shows something structural remains underneath.

**Skeptic:** The "p-hacking vs. deeper truth" framing is a false choice — soft theory is the mechanism that makes p-hacking invisible and consequence-free, not a rival explanation, and boring selection statistics (publish-only-if-significant plus ~50% power) can reproduce the entire crisis signature with zero misconduct required.
Pre-crisis "robustness" itself was never well measured — it passed through the same broken filter now under indictment, so part of the shock is manufactured by the filter, not evidence of a real fall from grace.

**Pragmatist:** Weight it roughly 60/40 toward fixable methods/incentives over deep theory, using the ~20-30 point gap between preregistered replication rates (~70-80%) and a hypothetical ceiling as a rough empirical ruler — then stop arguing philosophy and grade evidence by tier instead.
For a non-specialist, "how was this study run" (single lab vs. preregistered vs. multi-site) predicts trustworthiness better than picking a camp does.

**Critic:** Neither explanation survives alone, and neither applies uniformly — replication is bimodal (anchoring near-universal, many priming effects near-zero), so any single "% explained by X" number misrepresents a landscape that actually varies by finding.
"Deep epistemics" can curdle into an unfalsifiable alibi (an undemonstrated hidden moderator invoked only after failure) that gives cover to real misconduct, just as "just p-hacking" wrongly implies clean methods guarantee meaningful theory.

### Verdict
- **Consensus:** All four voices reject the question's either/or framing. QRPs/p-hacking are real, well-documented, and substantially remedied by preregistration — but that fix doesn't touch the deeper issue of weak, direction-only theorizing operating against a background of ambient correlation, which is what made QRPs so easy to commit and so hard to notice in the first place.
- **Strongest dissent:** Whether to quantify this at all. The Pragmatist wants a working number (~60/40) as a usable heuristic; the Skeptic and Critic both resist any single figure — the Skeptic because plain selection statistics (regression to the mean under a significance filter) may explain most of it without invoking "deep epistemics" at all, the Critic because the real answer is bimodal by finding and subfield, not a field-wide constant.
- **Premise check:** Yes — and not just the Skeptic. All three external voices independently converged on rejecting the dichotomy in the question. That three-way convergence is the strongest signal to come out of this council: the "is it A or B" framing was the thing most worth challenging.
- **Recommendation:** Give the layered answer, not a single cause. (1) QRPs and p-hacking were real and are the best-documented, most-fixed cause — the preregistration data proves it. (2) Underneath that, most psychological theories are "soft" (Meehl): they predict direction, not magnitude, against a backdrop of near-universal weak correlation, so a "significant" original finding was often cheap to get honestly, not only dishonestly. (3) Some failures are neither fraud nor bad theory but legitimate context-dependence (unrepresentative samples, hidden moderators) or noise in the replication attempt itself, not the original study. (4) The story isn't uniform — some effects (anchoring) are rock solid, others (many priming effects) were mostly mirage, and "the replication crisis," treated as one number, flattens a bimodal reality into a misleadingly single story.
