The "should be more strategic" framing is worth questioning before anything else. It assumes management is the strategic path and IC is the default/safe one — that's outdated. Most companies now have real staff/principal IC ladders with comparable comp and influence. Strategic just means optimizing for long-term leverage and optionality, not picking whichever track looks like a promotion from the outside.

The biggest piece of data you already have is the one you mentioned almost in passing: you like coding more than meetings. That's not a minor preference to override with willpower. As an IC, meetings are maybe a quarter of your time. As a manager, they're the majority of the job — it's not "more of the same," it's a different job that happens to share a job title with what you do now. People rarely grow to love work that runs against their energy just because they got promoted into it. Competence tracks with actually wanting to do the daily work, not with wanting the title.

One nuance worth being honest with yourself about: is it meetings in general you dislike, or specifically status-updates/coordination overhead? If it's the latter, management usually makes it worse, not better. If you've never minded mentoring or design discussions, that's a different signal. But if the honest answer is "I want to be building," that's real information, not immaturity.

You're also merging two separate decisions into one. First: IC vs. manager as a track. Second: stay vs. leave. These offers happen to be bundled (manager-at-current, IC-at-new), but that's a confound, not a clean comparison. Worth asking yourself: does your current company actually have a credible senior/staff IC ladder, or is management the only visible "up" there? A lot of manager offers exist because that's the only rung on the ladder, not because it's the best fit for that specific person. If there's no real IC path at your current company, staying put caps you either way.

There's also a reversibility argument, and 32 is exactly the age where it matters. Going IC now and moving into management later, if you want to, is the easy direction — the door stays open. Going into management now and stepping back to IC later is harder: hands-on skill decays fast when you're out of it for even a year or two, and there's a perception tax even though there shouldn't be. When genuinely torn, the lower-regret order is usually to stay IC, get excellent, and revisit management later from a position of choice rather than default.

Don't undervalue "slightly smaller" either. It often means more scope and ownership per engineer, closer proximity to product decisions, faster feedback — which is frequently better for a senior IC's growth than a bigger name. Weigh comp and stability honestly, but smaller isn't automatically a downgrade.

If you want one concrete way to break the tie: talk to an actual manager at your current company about what their week really looks like and whether they'd choose it again, and talk to a senior/staff-level IC at the new company about whether that track is genuinely valued there or a holding pen. Real conversations beat comparing abstract ladders.

My direct take: you told me you like coding more than meetings and that you're only eyeing management because you feel you "should." That's usually the tell. Absent a concrete pull toward staying — money, stability, a specific person you'd work for — that has nothing to do with ladder-guilt, take the senior IC offer. Taking a management job because it looks more strategic when you don't actually like the daily work of it is the standard way to become a mediocre manager who used to be a great engineer.
