The pulling-back pattern usually signals one of a few things, and they feel similar from the inside but point different directions.

The most common one is that you already know what you want but the answer feels socially or financially risky to admit. If every time you get close to saying yes you feel a faint grief about the autonomy, and every time you get close to saying no you feel relief rather than doubt — your gut is speaking clearly and your rational mind is finding reasons to override it. The "stability is appealing" framing is worth examining: appealing to what, exactly? If it's fear of a lean month rather than genuine desire for that life, stability is a weak anchor for a major decision.

The second thing that causes this is a real tie — both options are genuinely good, and you're waiting for one to pull clearly ahead, which it won't, because the information you need isn't going to arrive before you decide. In that case the indecision isn't protecting you from a mistake; it's just deferring a real trade-off indefinitely. The question then stops being "which is better?" and becomes "which regret can I live with?" Most freelancers who've built four years of autonomy regret surrendering it more than they regret declining a job offer. Most people who've never had it regret not having tried. That asymmetry matters.

The third is that the decision feels binary when it isn't quite. Before committing either way, it's worth knowing: could you negotiate a part-time arrangement, a contract-to-hire with a defined exit clause, or a retained relationship if you stay freelance? Sometimes the oscillation is the mind trying to tell you that neither option as presented is right, and there's a third shape to the deal you haven't asked for yet.

The specific thing to notice is what you're doing when you pull back. Are you pulling back toward freelancing — actively excited about what you'd be protecting? Or are you just stepping away from the discomfort of committing? Those look identical on the surface but mean opposite things. The first is a preference. The second is avoidance, and avoidance resolves when you pick a deadline and hold to it regardless of the emotional weather that day.
