## Answer

The move is probably worth taking, but only if you treat it as a defined chapter, not an open-ended drift.

**The real question isn't "city A vs city B." It's: what does a 2-4 year stint of higher income actually cost you, and is that cost real?**

Work through it this way:

**1. Quantify the money concretely, not abstractly.** 40% on "just okay" comp is the most important variable here. Run the actual numbers: what's the annual delta after tax and cost-of-living adjustment (the new city might be cheaper or more expensive)? If it's $20k/year net, that's real but modest. If it's $40k+, that's a genuine wealth-building opportunity that changes your options for years afterward — higher base anchors all future offers.

**2. Separate "long-term" from "2-4 years."** You said you don't want to live there *long-term*. That's different from "I can't tolerate it for 2 years." Most people who move for career reasons with a clear exit plan find it manageable. People who move with no plan and just hope it works out find themselves stuck at year 5. **So: have the exit conversation before you accept.** "We'd go for 2-3 years, save aggressively, then move to [city X]." If that plan feels concrete and both you and your partner can commit to it, the move is probably rational.

**3. Is this a career step or just a pay step?** 40% more doing the exact same thing in a new city is worth less than 40% more at a company/role that opens doors. Ask: does this job accelerate your career in ways current company can't?

**4. "Partner is flexible" needs more precision.** Flexible isn't enthusiastic. Before deciding, have a real conversation about what flexible actually means — will they resent it in year 2? Do they have a career/social life that moves easily, or are they leaving a lot behind?

**The actual decision heuristic:** If you can answer yes to all four — (a) the net dollar delta is material, (b) you have a real exit plan to the right city, (c) the job is a genuine step forward, (d) your partner is genuinely on board — take it. If two or more are no, hold out for something better or negotiate remote/relocation flexibility.

The economic case for moving is real, but "ambient wrong city" is a draining tax paid in small daily doses — the commute through neighborhoods you don't like, the weekends with nothing you want to do, the slow erosion of feeling like you're living your actual life. At 29, with compounding social roots starting to form, two or three years in the wrong place can cost more in wellbeing than the salary gain buys back. The reversibility argument assumes you'll actually leave — but lifestyle inflation, inertia, and career lock-in mean a surprising number of "2-year plans" become 6. If the city genuinely doesn't fit you (climate, culture, distance from family, whatever the real issue is), price that honestly rather than rationalizing it away.
