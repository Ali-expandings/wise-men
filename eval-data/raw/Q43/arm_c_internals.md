## MEMBER: Utilitarian
Favors proactive low-pressure acknowledgment over ignoring or silent-planning. Suppressing info = bad equilibria (suboptimal staffing, no retention chance, trust erosion). Frame as career conversation not interrogation ("want to make sure you're getting what you need"). Risks: confrontational framing backfires; chilling effect on others; false-positive harm (signal may be wrong); silent planning leaks → resentment/accelerated exit. Rec: general proactive career convo with ALL reports + quiet contingency planning as standard practice; don't say "I heard you're job searching" unless ready to name the source. Conf med-high 75%. Weakest: assumes employee retainable — if reasons structural, "open door" yields only shorter planning horizon, tilt to quiet planning + graceful off-ramp.

## MEMBER: Deontologist
Duty to treat report as rational agent with full autonomy over career. Job searching = legitimate self-determination, not a breach. Acting on private knowledge to disadvantage them (deprioritize, quietly build replacement) uses them merely as means = Kantian violation. Universalizability: if all managers acted on secret job-search signals, employment trust collapses. Risks: silent planning punishes an undisclosed act; bringing up uninvited = surveillance/coercion of disclosure; doing-nothing-but-resenting leaks into decisions. Rec: do NOT bring it up unless they do; do NOT restructure to disadvantage; do what you should always do — honest recurring career conversations with all reports; discharge continuity duty by building resilient teams generally not targeted suppression. Conf high on framing. Weakest: assumes non-disclosure is autonomous choice not fear-based — if culture unsafe, upstream fix is make-it-safe.

## MEMBER: Virtue Ethicist
Phronimos doesn't weaponize private knowledge nor feign blindness when sight serves both. Quietly planning while feigning ignorance = double-dealing, corrupts relational honesty. Virtuous path: create honest conditions for the conversation without forcing disclosure; act from the disposition you should already have, not reactively from the stumbled-on secret. Risks: direct "I know you're searching" violates the trust by which you learned it; silent planning instrumentalizes; willful ignorance = moral cowardice dressed as neutrality. Rec: proactive genuine career conversation today (what's engaging you / where to grow / anything different); if disclosed plan openly, if not you gave the opening + may plan contingency clean-conscience. Virtues: justice + courage. Conf high structural / moderate framing. Weakest: assumes relationship healthy enough to absorb candor.

## MEMBER: Rights Advocate
Report has legitimate right to privacy in job search; non-disclosure is a deliberate choice, not an omission you're owed. Acting on the info without their knowledge = power asymmetry (you hold intel they don't know you have, can use against their interests). Silent planning carries most ethical weight — deciding about their career on info they never consented to you using. Risks: direct → forces lie or premature disclosure (coercive, signals surveillance); silent → treats unverified inference as fact, may disadvantage on something untrue, no ability to contest; act-like-you-don't-know → lowest violation but needs discipline or becomes covert planning with extra steps. Rec: act like you don't know BUT make driving conditions visible/addressable via genuine uncoded retention conversation; don't make resource/project decisions on the inference; manage departure when real. Conf mod-high rights framing. Weakest: that your inference is even correct.

## MEMBER: Devil's Advocate
Confront directly and early. The "quiet career conversation" approach fails the most important stakeholder: you. Silent planning treats the problem as static; if they're good enough to worry about, they're good enough for an honest adult conversation. CONCRETE FAILURE — the Cascade: you plan quietly → they see work redistributed/projects frozen/growth deprioritized → read it (correctly) as being written off → search accelerates "exploring"→"urgent" → leave in 6 weeks not 6 months → you lose transition time because silent planning telegraphed itself; other reports notice, anxiety spreads, new retention problem. Silent approach assumes the other person is passive. They are not. Rec: open with honesty not indirection ("I've noticed signals you might be thinking about what's next; not cornering you, want to be useful + plan well; you don't owe me anything but I'd rather talk openly"). Reframes accusation→partnership, gives face-saving exit if wrong. Conf high 7/10 (context-dependent; toxic org lowers it). Weakest: assumes enough relational trust that "direct" doesn't read as threatening/retaliatory — if they're searching because of YOU, could trigger HR complaint.
