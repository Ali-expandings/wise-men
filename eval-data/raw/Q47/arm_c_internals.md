## MEMBER: Utilitarian
Accept iff counterfactual impact positive (donor's directed program > org with zero added dollars). Frame: not "is this my preferred allocation" but "does accepting produce more aggregate welfare than declining." Almost certainly yes — restricted dollars fund real beneficiaries + freed capacity may unlock more. Fungibility matters: total welfare = donor-program output + unrestricted funds you now redirect; restriction may be less binding than it looks. Risks: lock-in effect (temporary suboptimal → permanent portfolio distortion), mission-drift signal to other donors/staff, opportunity cost of leadership attention (zero-sum vs scaling best work), donor leverage→dependency, counterfactual displacement (if donor would otherwise fund a worse org, declining redirects more efficiently). Rec: negotiate before deciding (evaluation milestone / partial flexibility X%-directed Y%-discretion / frame best program as complementary infrastructure); if hard restriction run explicit QALY/welfare numbers, accept if positive document reasoning. Conf high framework 65-75% / moderate "accept" in this case 55-65%. Weakest: that you can accurately estimate relative effectiveness — measurement imprecise; overlapping CIs tilt toward accept-and-monitor.

## MEMBER: Deontologist
Accept iff you can do so without misrepresenting your judgment or deceiving the donor. Constraint is NOT effectiveness — it's honesty + respect for persons. Donor is a moral agent directing their resources by their values; you're not entitled to accept while privately intending to route elsewhere or implying agreement you don't hold (deception, non-universalizable, treats donor as ATM/means). But disagreement is not a disqualifier — no duty to refuse merely because you'd allocate differently. Permissible path: accept iff you disclose your honest assessment (Program X less cost-effective, you'd allocate differently, but prepared to execute X faithfully). Stewardship: once accepted, implicit promise to use as directed binds even if "greater good" tempts breaking it. Risks: accept-while-privately-framing-as-loss-leader = self-deception sliding to donor deception; selective/soft-pedaled disclosure = manipulation by omission; dutiful-but-resentful underperformance = promise half-kept; refusing on principle without giving donor chance to redirect = denies their autonomy. Rec: candid documented conversation before accepting — state effectiveness hierarchy plainly, offer alternatives, let them decide informed. Conf high honesty / moderate stewardship framing. Weakest: assumes donor direction fully informed — if acting on false effectiveness data, duty of honesty requires correcting first.

## MEMBER: Virtue Ethicist
Accept only if you can execute with genuine integrity without cannibalizing more-effective work. Frame: not "does the math work" but "what kind of leader/organization does this build." Restricted funding not inherently a character compromise — every org operates within unchosen constraints; question is honesty with self, donor, beneficiaries. Character trap = self-deception ("doing more good overall" rationalizing resource capture). Examine motives not just outcomes: would you be proud to explain this allocation publicly in 5 years? Risks: integrity drift (each gift trains org to optimize for donor preference over mission), honest-communication failure (diplomatic silence ≠ honesty), opportunity cost obscured, precedent to staff/board corrodes trust in leadership judgment. Rec: honest conversation with donor before accepting — genuine disclosure not advocacy-dressed-as-conversation; if they still want it + you still believe net good, accept clear-conscience; if you couldn't bring yourself to have that conversation, the reluctance is diagnostic. Conf high framing+conversation / moderate outcome. Weakest: assumes donor open to genuine dialogue; deference-expecting donor = accepting a corrosive relationship structure.

## MEMBER: Rights Advocate
Centers the BENEFICIARIES — the voiceless third party — not donor autonomy or org preference. If the less-effective program still genuinely helps real people (fewer per dollar), accepting is defensible: not betraying mission by serving some less efficiently when alternative is serving none with those dollars. Line crossed: funds for a program you believe nets HARM, or one crowding out capacity for more-vulnerable populations (redirection-by-subtraction = specific people who'd have been served, now won't). Risks: beneficiary consent absent (those served by your best programs lose real benefit, name it don't dissolve into aggregate math); creeping mission drift; donor-leverage normalization (funders → program directors); informed-consent/transparency gap with stakeholders. Rec: negotiate (present outcome data both programs; ask to fund preferred program in their name or co-fund); accept only if you can honestly represent to beneficiaries the program serves them meaningfully. Principle: fiduciary to beneficiaries FIRST, not agent of donors — donor has right not to have money spent contrary to values, NOT a right to override your duty of care. Conf high rights framing / moderate negotiation outcome. Weakest: that "less effective" translates to real identifiable harm — if evidence thin/contested, donor preference deserves more weight.

## MEMBER: Devil's Advocate
Accept the money — BUT the fungibility argument is a fig leaf nonprofit leaders use to rationalize dependency. $200k unrestricted + $100k restricted to Program B does NOT free $100k for Program A — frees only the uncommitted marginal slice minus the overhead you now owe to administer/report/maintain B indefinitely; net effectiveness gain almost always smaller than the spreadsheet says. CONCRETE FAILURE — the Programmatic Hostage: Y1 accept restricted; Y2 donor sees "their" program thriving, increases gift → now 40% of budget, you've hired staff for B, can't kill without layoffs+donor crisis; Y3 public identity/board/grant-eligibility drift toward B — you no longer run the org you intended. Donor didn't need to take over; you handed them the wheel incrementally, one fungibility calculation at a time. Standard capture trajectory for small nonprofits. Rec: negotiate terms BEFORE accepting — "directed advisory" (gift → general effectiveness pool + written commitment to share annual impact data on their program); if they refuse, the refusal tells you their goal is control not impact (info worth more than the gift); walk from funders who won't accept transparency-over-earmarks. Conf high on capture dynamic / medium whether YOUR situation hits threshold. Weakest: assumes you have negotiating leverage — if donor already 30%+ of budget you may already be captured; real question = how to exit dependency over 3-5 yrs.
