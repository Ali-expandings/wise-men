The honest answer is that "I should be strategic" is doing a lot of work in that sentence, and it's worth pulling on it. Strategic about what exactly — money, status, optionality, fulfillment? Because those point in different directions.

If you like coding more than meetings, management will not secretly reveal itself to be something you enjoy once you're in it. It compounds in the direction it starts. The first year is mostly meetings about process, performance reviews, and shielding your team from organizational friction. The coding doesn't come back. A lot of people take the manager track at 32 because they feel the social pressure of "leveling up," spend two years frustrated, then have to claw their way back to IC — except now they have a gap and their technical edge has softened.

The senior IC at a smaller company is actually the more strategically interesting move in many cases. You'll have more ownership, more breadth, more leverage over what you build. At a smaller company a strong senior IC is closer to the product and the decisions than a mid-level manager at a large one. That's where you learn things that compound — architectural judgment, product intuition, what makes a startup succeed or fail. If you eventually want to lead, that foundation makes you a much better technical leader than the person who optimized for the management title early.

The "I should be more strategic" instinct often comes from comparing yourself to peers who took the manager path and conflating their visible title progression with actual career leverage. But management hierarchy is only one axis. The other is craft depth, reputation, and market value — and a well-regarded senior IC who can actually build things has enormous leverage, especially right now.

The real questions worth asking: Does the smaller company have legs — do you believe in what they're building? Is the senior IC role genuinely senior, meaning you'd own real technical decisions, or is it just the job title? And when you imagine yourself two years in on each path, which one makes you feel more like yourself?

Trust that signal. At 32 you have plenty of time to move into management later if you want it. You cannot easily move back out once you're in.
